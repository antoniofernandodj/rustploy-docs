In about 15 minutes you will put an application online, watch the zero-downtime swap happen and go
back a version. We will use the public `nginx` image, so you do not depend on any code of yours.

**Before you start** you need the [daemon installed](/en/instalacao/instalar-daemon/) and a
[client](/en/instalacao/instalar-clientes/). The button names below are the web UI's. In the
[desktop app](/en/interface/visao-geral/) the **Application** wizard only asks for the name: the source,
port and domain are configured afterwards, on the service's **General** tab.

## 1. Sign in

Open the dashboard and paste the [connection token](/en/instalacao/primeiro-acesso/). You land on the
**Deployments** screen, still empty.

## 2. Create a project

A project is the group of services that see each other on a network of their own.

<Steps>
1. In the side menu, open **Projects**.
2. Click **+ Novo projeto** (New project), name it `tutorial` and confirm.
3. Click the project's card to open it.
</Steps>

## 3. Create a service

<Steps>
1. On the project's **Serviços** (Services) tab, click **+ Novo serviço** (New service).
2. Under *Escolha o tipo de serviço* (Choose the service type), select **Application**.
3. Fill in:
   - **Nome** (Name): `site`
   - **Origem** (Source): the **Registry** tab, with the image `nginx:latest`
   - **Porta** (Port): `80`, the port nginx listens on *inside* the container
   - **Domínio** (Domain): leave it blank for now
4. Click **Criar Application** (Create Application).
</Steps>

The service was **created, but it is not online yet**: creating does not deploy. You landed on the
service screen, with the tabs **General, Connection, Environment, Domains, Deployments, Healthcheck,
Logs and Advanced**.

## 4. Configure the healthcheck

The healthcheck is what authorizes the swap. Without it, Rustploy only guarantees that the container
started, not that the application responds.

<Steps>
1. Open the **Healthcheck** tab.
2. Under **KIND** choose **Http**.
3. Under **HTTP PATH** use `/` and under **EXPECTED STATUS** `200`.
4. Click **Save**.
</Steps>

A new service starts with the healthcheck set to **None**. Always configure one before real use.

## 5. Deploy

Click **Deploy** at the top of the service. Open the **Deployments** tab and follow the states. For a
registry image, the path is:

```text
Pending → PullingImage → Staging → HealthcheckPolling → SwappingIn → Draining → Promoting → Live
```

In short: Rustploy pulls the image, starts a container **alongside** the old one (the *staging*), tests
the healthcheck, swaps the proxy route and, only then, removes the old container (if any). When the
state reaches **Live**, the service is online. The service status becomes **Running**.

See what the container writes on the **Logs** tab.

## 6. Point a domain

<Aside type="caution" title="DNS first">
  Create a DNS `A` record for your domain (for example `site.mysite.com`) pointing at the server's IP
  **before** this step. For HTTPS, [ACME must be on](/en/instalacao/instalar-daemon/#turn-on-https) and
  port 80 reachable.
</Aside>

<Steps>
1. Open the **Domains** tab.
2. Type the domain in `dominio.com`. Leave the port blank (it uses the service's port, `80`).
3. Tick **TLS** for automatic HTTPS and click **Adicionar** (Add).
</Steps>

Visit `https://site.mysite.com`. The first visit may take a few seconds while the certificate is
issued. Without TLS ticked, the service answers over HTTP on the ingress port (80 by default).

Each domain appears in the list as `https → :80` or `http → :80`: the public scheme and the container
port the traffic goes to.

## 7. Do a second deploy and watch the swap

Change something that makes a difference, for example the image to `nginx:alpine` on the **General**
tab, save, and click **Deploy** again.

This time the old container keeps serving while the new one starts and passes its healthcheck. The route
only changes at the **SwappingIn** step, all at once; then comes **Draining** (a short wait, 10 s by
default, for in-flight connections to finish) and, at **Promoting**, the removal of the old one. If the
new one's healthcheck failed, the state would go to **RollingBack** and the old one would stay online.

## 8. Go back a version

Today the reliable way back is to **redeploy the previous version**: on the **General** tab, put back
the image (or branch, or commit) that worked and click **Deploy**. Like every deploy, it only swaps the
route after passing the healthcheck.

<Aside type="caution" title="The Rollback button">
  The service's **Rollback** button only locates the previous deploy; it does not redeploy. Do not
  count on it to go back a version.
</Aside>

## What you learned

- A project groups services on a network.
- Creating a service does not deploy; **Deploy** does.
- The healthcheck decides whether the swap happens.
- The proxy switches the route all at once, with no window of unavailability.
- Domain and TLS live on the **Domains** tab.

## If something went wrong

| Symptom | What to look at |
|---|---|
| The deploy ended in **Failed** | **Deployments** tab: the cause appears in the deploy's log. See [The lifecycle of a deploy](/en/conceitos/ciclo-de-vida/) |
| The domain returns **502** | The proxy has the route, but the container did not respond. Check the domain's **port** and the healthcheck. An AI agent can help: [diagnose 502](/en/agentes/receitas/) |
| The certificate is not issued | DNS does not point at the server yet, ACME is off, or port 80 is closed |
| Nothing answers on the domain, over HTTP | Check that the service is **Running** and that the ingress `http_port` is the port you opened |
