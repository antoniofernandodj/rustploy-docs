A service can have **several domains**, each with its own container port and with or without TLS. Rustploy's
proxy (the ingress) routes by domain name.

## First: DNS and ACME

<Steps>
1. Create the domain's DNS `A` (or `AAAA`) record pointing at the server's IP. Rustploy **does not manage DNS**.
2. For HTTPS, turn on ACME in `config.toml` and restart the daemon:

   ```toml
   [ingress.acme]
   enabled = true
   email   = "you@example.com"
   ```

3. Make sure ports **80** and **443** are reachable from the internet.
</Steps>

## Add a domain

On the service's **Domains** tab:

- **dominio.com**: the name (`app.mysite.com`);
- **porta (opcional)** (port, optional): the container port that receives the traffic. Blank uses the service's port;
- **TLS**: tick it for HTTPS.

Click **Adicionar** (Add). The list shows each route as `https → :8080` or `http → :80`: the public scheme
and the container port.

You can point several domains at the same service, even at **different ports** of the same container.

## How the certificate is issued

When a service with a **TLS** domain finishes a deploy (`Live`), the daemon requests the certificate from
Let's Encrypt in the background, using the HTTP-01 challenge, and loads it without restarting. So:

- the **first access** may take a few seconds, or fail right after the first deploy, until the certificate is issued;
- **renewal** is automatic, in the background;
- certificates live in `<db_path>/certs/<domain>/`.

<Aside type="caution" title="If the certificate is not issued">
  The causes, from most to least common: DNS does not point at the server yet; `ingress.acme.enabled` is
  `false` or has no `email`; port 80 is blocked (Let's Encrypt needs it); or the domain was never attempted
  because the service did not reach `Live`.
</Aside>

## Without TLS

Without ticking **TLS**, the domain answers over HTTP on the ingress port (`80` by default), with no
certificate. It suits tests and internal networks.

## Host TCP port (databases and brokers)

Services that are **not HTTP** do not use a domain: they get a **TCP port on the server** that forwards straight
to the container. On the **Domains** tab, the **PORTA TCP DO HOST** (host TCP port) block has **Salvar porta**
(Save port, you pick the number) and **Automática** (Automatic, Rustploy picks one from the `[external_ports]`
range, default 20000–20999, and opens it in the firewall). The port appears under **Host port** and
**External URL** on the **Connection** tab. This exposes the service to the internet: use a strong password.
See [Server security](/en/operacao/seguranca-do-servidor/).
