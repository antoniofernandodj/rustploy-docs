A **job** is a task that runs until it finishes and then goes away: a backup, a migration, a report, a cleanup.
It is not a service that stays online. Each run starts its own Docker Compose stack, on the **project's
network**, and removes it at the end.

Jobs appear on the **Schedules** screen (all projects) and on each project's **Jobs** tab.

## Create a job

<Steps>
1. In **Schedules** (or on the project's **Jobs** tab), click **+ Novo job** (New job).
2. Choose the **project**.
3. Choose the **trigger service**, or **Nenhum (autônomo)** (None, standalone). The trigger only lends its environment variables to the job; the job **does not run inside** its container.
4. Fill in the form and confirm.
</Steps>

### The form

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| **NOME** (Name) | for example `daily-backup` |
| **FONTE DO COMPOSE** (Compose source) | **Compose** (you paste the YAML) or **Git** (the job clones a repository and runs its `docker-compose.yml` on every run) |
| **MAIN SERVICE** | the compose service whose **exit code** decides whether the job succeeded (`docker compose up --exit-code-from`) |
| **VARIÁVEIS DE AMBIENTE** (Environment variables) | the job's own, in `.env` format. They have the **highest precedence**, above the project's and the trigger service's |
| **Quando** (When) | **Manual**, **Intervalo** (Interval), **Diário** (Daily) or **Semanal** (Weekly) |

With the **Git** source, choose the account (connected in **Settings → Git**), the repository, the branch and
the **compose path** inside it.

### Example

```yaml
services:
  job:
    image: postgres:16
    command: pg_dump -h rp_database -U app -f /backup/dump.sql app
    volumes:
      - /var/backups:/backup
```

With `MAIN SERVICE` = `job`. If `pg_dump` exits with a code other than 0, the run counts as a failure.

## Scheduling

| Type | Fields |
|---|---|
| **Manual** | no schedule: it only fires when you ask |
| **Intervalo** | **A CADA (HORAS)** (every N hours) |
| **Diário** | **HORÁRIO** (time) |
| **Semanal** | **DIA DA SEMANA** (weekday) and **HORÁRIO** |

<Aside type="caution" title="The time is in UTC">
  The daemon interprets the **HORÁRIO** in **UTC**, with no conversion to your time zone, and the field does
  not say so. In Brazil (UTC−3), "03:00" fires at 00:00 local time. Add your offset when you choose. Where the
  screen shows the **next run**, it is in local time: use it to check.
</Aside>

## A job's actions

The **⋯** button on the row opens the actions panel:

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **▶ Rodar agora** (Run now) | runs right away, outside the schedule |
| **■ Cancelar execução** (Cancel run) | interrupts the run in progress |
| **◐ Aquecer** (Warm) | prepares the job without running it (see below) |
| **≡ Ver logs** (View logs) | output (stdout and stderr) of the runs |
| **✎ Editar** (Edit) | changes the job |
| **✕ Remover** (Remove) | deletes the job |

Jobs and deploys share the **same queue**: one at a time on the server. A manual job may wait its turn behind a deploy.

## Warm a job

A job's first run downloads images and builds everything from scratch. That can happen at the worst moment
(in the middle of a deploy waiting on a *migrator*, or at night). **Warming** downloads the images and builds the
cache **without running** the task.

The job shows as **quente** (warm), **frio** (cold) or **aquecendo…** (warming…). It is **cold** when:

- it was never warmed;
- the definition changed (compose, repository, branch or path);
- an image disappeared from the host;
- the build cache was deleted by a Docker cleanup.

## If the daemon restarts

Runs in progress (or waiting in the queue) become **interrupted**: they are marked as failed, with code `-1` and
a line in the log explaining. The scheduler fires them again at the next time.
